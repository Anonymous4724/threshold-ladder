About Threshold Ladder

Threshold Ladder answers one question competitive Fortnite players ask before and during every cup: how many points will it take to reach this rank?

Why it exists

Cutoffs are published only after the fact. A player deciding whether to keep queuing, how many risks to take in the last games, or whether a cup is worth entering at all is guessing – usually from a vague memory of last week's number, read off a leaderboard at the wrong time of the evening. The data to do better exists: every finished leaderboard is public. This site turns it into a forecast with an honest range, for every cup of the week, in every region.

What it does

  • A forecast for every cup of the week at any rank, before the cup starts, from the cup's own history, or from recent leaderboards replayed under its scoring table when it is new (this week's cups).
  • A live forecast while the cup runs, from the public standings read every five minutes and weighed against the history.
  • Guides to what sets a cutoff, written from the same data (all guides).

Everything the forecast computes happens in your browser. There is no account, and a tournament you type in never leaves your device.

Who makes it

Threshold Ladder is an independent fan project, made by a Fortnite player who likes numbers and publishes the code under the name Anonymous4724 on GitHub. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Epic Games or Osirion.

The model and its measurements are open source, and every change to the model is measured on tournaments it had not seen before it ships; the method page summarises how, and links to the full notes.

Data and credits

Tournament calendars, scoring rules and leaderboards come from Osirion's public Fortnite API. The site publishes forecasts and statistics derived from them; the full standings of any cup are on osirion.gg. The model is rebuilt every day, the calendar of the week every three hours.
